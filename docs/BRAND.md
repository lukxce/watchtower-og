# Watchtower — Brand Platform

*Owner: Luka Jovanović · Last revised: 18 August 2026 · Supersedes the "first light" brand doc*

---

## 1. The story

A watchtower exists for one reason: **someone stands the watch so everyone else can sleep.**

It doesn't shout. It doesn't light the beacon for a fox on the road. It watches everything, understands what it sees, and lights the fire only when something real is moving. And when that fire is lit you trust it — because the tower has never lied to you.

That is the product, almost literally. Watchtower stands the watch over your competitors — their pricing, their launches, their hiring, their ads, the hostnames they haven't announced — reads what it sees, and brings you conclusions in the morning. What they did. Why it matters. What to do about it.

Never a raw pile of alerts. Never a false fire.

### The two-beat lore

> **Scouts gather. The Tower sees.**

This is not decoration; it is the architecture, and that is why it holds up.

- **Scouts** are the individual collectors — one per public channel. Pricing pages, ad libraries, job boards, certificate-transparency logs, press, review sites, sitemaps, events pages. Each one goes out, brings back what it found, and reports honestly when it found nothing.
- **The Tower** is the product. It sees everything the scouts bring back, reads it *together* per competitor, and writes the briefing.

The distinction earns its keep in the UI: a scout's report is a fact with a source. The Tower's read is a conclusion. We never blur them, and the customer can always see which is which.

### Why the metaphor works (and where it stops)

It works because a watchtower is **defensive, patient and honest**. It is not a spy, not a hacker, not a growth-hack. Everything Watchtower reads is public — it is simply read continuously, and read *together*, which almost nobody does.

It stops at anything that implies **secrecy, intrusion or aggression**. We are not "surveillance." We are not "spying on your competitors." We do not do cloak-and-dagger, dark-mode-hacker aesthetics, crosshairs, or targets. Two reasons: it is legally and ethically the wrong signal for a product that only reads public data, and it is the exact register every mediocre competitor already uses.

**Cardinal rule: we lean medieval-structural, never military-tactical.** Stone, courses, elevation, watch, beacon, frontier, first light. Not sniper, target, kill-shot, war room, weaponize.

## 2. Positioning

### Positioning statement

> For **B2B teams who have to act on what their competitors do** — sales, product marketing, founders — **Watchtower** is a **competitive-intelligence platform** that **watches every public channel continuously and returns one briefing that says what changed, why it matters and what to do**. Unlike **alert tools that forward raw page-diffs, or enterprise CI suites that cost five figures and still need a full-time analyst**, Watchtower **reads signals together, cites every claim, and starts at $99 a month.**

### The three-sentence version (for a call)

Most teams find out a competitor cut their price or shipped a feature from a lost deal, three weeks late. The tools that exist either forward raw detections you still have to interpret, or cost fifteen thousand a year and need someone to run them. Watchtower watches 22 public channels a day, reads them together, and writes the briefing — every fact cited, from $99.

### Category

We compete in **competitive intelligence**, and we should not try to invent a category. The buyer already searches for "competitive intelligence software," "Crayon alternative," "Klue pricing." Inventing a name means fighting for a term nobody types.

What we *do* claim inside the category is a stance: **verifiable competitive intelligence.** That adjective is the wedge, and it is defensible because it is enforced in code.

### The competitive frame

| | Their position | Our counter |
|---|---|---|
| **Klue** | Enterprise CI, sales-enablement led. Strong battlecards, deep CRM integration. Quote after a demo call. | We publish pricing. You are live today, not after procurement. |
| **Crayon** | The category's brand name. Broad tracking, enterprise motion, expensive. | Same coverage class, an order of magnitude cheaper, and every claim cites a source instead of a confidence score. |
| **Kompyte** | Acquired by Semrush; now runs on Semrush's distribution rather than as an independent product. | We are a company whose only job is this. Independence is a feature when the roadmap is someone else's. |
| **Visualping** | Page-change monitoring. Cheap, useful, and genuinely good at one channel. | Page changes are one of 22 channels, and a change is not an insight. We tell you what it means. |
| **Signal Labs** | Newer, quotes Team tier on a call. | Published pricing, live demo, no gate. |
| **A shared Notion doc** | Free. **This is the real competitor.** | It is six months stale and you know it. |

**Do not punch down at Visualping.** They are honest about being a monitoring tool. Our contrast is with the *enterprise-priced* incumbents, and with the status quo of doing it by hand.

### What we will not claim

- We will not claim to predict the future. Launch Radar is a **forecast with named evidence**, not a prophecy.
- We will not claim AI magic. The reasoning layer is grounded in retrieved facts, and we say so.
- We will not claim completeness we cannot prove. If a page can't be fetched, the product says so. That honesty *is* the marketing.

## 3. The brand laws

These are enforced in the codebase, not just the style guide. Three laws, in priority order.

### Law 1 — The tower reads, you decide

Everything shown to a customer is a **conclusion in plain language** — "Klue looks to be building something new" — never an artifact of how we found it — "Subdomain observed: interviewer-v2.klue.com". Detection plumbing is our business, not theirs. The observation stays underneath as the how-we-know line.

### Law 2 — The beacon is earned

Loud treatment — the highlighter, size, prominence — is reserved for things that clear an evidence bar. Most of the interface stays calm. When the highlighter appears it means *look here, this is real*. A UI where everything is urgent has no way to say "this one matters."

### Law 3 — No false fires

Every conclusion carries its source. Anything unverifiable is skipped and logged, and the product says so out loud. A competitor "raised funding" always links to the article that says so.

**Corollary, added August 2026:** never surface a bare keyword match. A brand name that matches a song, a band or a farmhouse is not a mention. Every match is classified — *is this the client, a different entity of the same name, or noise?* — and when there is nothing to disambiguate against, we say that rather than pretending.

## 4. Audience, and how we speak to them

### Who we are talking to

| Segment | What keeps them up | What they need from the first screen |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder / CEO** (10–150 staff) | Being the last to know. A competitor's move showing up in a board meeting. | The state of the market in ten seconds, without assigning anyone to compile it. |
| **Product marketing** (often a team of one) | Battlecards going stale the week after they're written. Being asked "what's new with X" and not knowing. | Cards that maintain themselves, with sources they can forward to sales. |
| **Sales lead / enablement** | Reps losing deals on an objection nobody briefed them on. | "How we win against X," in language a rep can use on a call. |
| **Product** | Discovering a competitor shipped the thing on your roadmap. | Early buildout signals — hostnames, hiring clusters — before the launch post. |

### How we address them

**Operator to operator.** The reader is competent, busy, and has been sold to badly many times. They do not need to be excited; they need to be respected and then informed.

- Assume intelligence. Do not explain what a competitor is.
- Lead with the finding, not the framing. "Klue is shipping an AI interviewer. They haven't said so." — then the evidence.
- Second person, active voice. "You'll know the morning it happens," not "users are notified."
- Specific beats impressive. "Three hostnames on the certificate log" is worth more than "advanced signal detection."
- Never manufacture urgency. The product's whole promise is that it only shouts when it's real — marketing that shouts constantly contradicts the product.

### Voice: do and don't

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "Leverage AI-powered competitive insights to supercharge your GTM." | "Know the moment they cut a price." |
| "9 signals ingested across the jobs channel." | "They opened nine technical roles this month." |
| "Unlock unparalleled market visibility." | "Most of your market happens in the dark." |
| "Our proprietary algorithm detects…" | "It turned up on the public certificate log." |
| "Never miss a beat!" | "Stop being the last to know." |
| "Trusted by industry leaders" (with no names) | Name them, or say nothing. |

### Tone by surface

- **Homepage** — confident, concrete, a little wry. It can have a sense of humour; it must never be silly.
- **In the product** — calm and factual. The product is read at 8am by someone who hasn't had coffee. No exclamation marks, no celebration.
- **Empty states** — honest and useful. "Nothing clears the evidence bar right now" is a feature, said plainly.
- **Errors and gaps** — direct, never cute. "We couldn't reach this page" beats "Oops!"
- **Blog** — this is where the personality lives. First-person, opinionated, evidence-led. See the content strategy doc.

## 5. Naming system

A consistent internal vocabulary that surfaces in the product and the marketing. Use these words; don't invent synonyms.

| Term | Means | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Tower** | The reasoning layer / the product's voice | Capital T. Never "the AI." Never "the Keeper" (retired). |
| **Scouts** | The individual channel collectors | Plural, lowercase. |
| **The watch** | The continuous daily collection | "Standing the watch." |
| **First light** | The morning briefing moment | Used as a timestamp label in the feed. |
| **Beacon** | The highlighter accent, used only for real signal | Both a visual token and a concept. |
| **Fog of war** | The unlit part of your market | Used on the homepage as an interaction. Preferred over "the frontier" — people already have a feeling about it. |
| **Battlecard** | The per-competitor briefing | Industry-standard term; keep it. |
| **Launch Radar** | Forecast of who is about to ship | Fires only on corroborating signal types. |
| **Threat Index** | The per-competitor score | Always shown with its inputs, never as a bare number. |

**Retired, do not reuse:** "the Keeper" (the AI's old name), "the frontier" (as a section label), "signal indigo" / "first light" as *palette* names.

## 6. Taglines

### Primary

> **Know the moment they move.**

Short, active, and the literal product promise. The homepage runs a rotating tail on it — *know the moment they **cut a price / mention you / ship something / launch an ad / get a review / open roles / raise a round / go quiet*** — which demonstrates breadth without a feature list. That rotation is a brand asset; keep it.

### Supporting lines

| Line | Use |
|---|---|
| **Scouts gather. The Tower sees.** | The lore line. Section headers, about page, careers, the product's own voice. |
| **Stop being the last to know.** | Closing CTA. Loss-framed, so use it at the end, never the top. |
| **Every fact cited.** | The trust line. Pairs with any claim about coverage. |
| **Most of your market happens in the dark.** | The problem statement. Opens the fog-of-war section. |
| **No false fires.** | Internal law used externally when talking about accuracy. |
| **Published pricing, because we're not afraid of it.** | Pricing page. A direct shot at the demo-gate incumbents. |
| **It watches. It reads. It tells you what to do.** | The three-beat product explanation. |

### Rules

- The primary tagline always pairs with the logo in the lockup position. Supporting lines never do.
- Don't stack two taglines in one viewport. One idea per screen.
- "Know the moment they move" is the only line that should ever appear without context.

## 7. Visual identity

### The mark — "Offset courses"

Three courses of stone with staggered joints, the way masonry is actually laid.

```
 ████████████        ← top course, highlighter
     ████████████    ← middle, 70% ink
 ████████████        ← base, 42% ink
```

The **offset is load-bearing**, not a stylistic choice. Centred bars read as a bar chart; a centred stack under a dot reads as a person. The 4-unit stagger is what makes it read as *structure* — a tower, built, solid.

**Construction:** three rects, 16 units wide, 6 tall, 1.4 corner radius, on a 34×34 viewBox. Top at x=7, middle at x=11, base at x=7. Vertical rhythm 5.5 / 14 / 22.5.

**Colour behaviour:** the structure is `currentColor` at descending opacity so the mark inverts anywhere. **Only the top course takes the highlighter** — it is the lit course, the beacon, and it is the single place the accent appears in the lockup.

**Lockup:** mark + "watchtower" set as one word, tight. Mark 30px against 17px type in the nav; the footer runs a size larger (35px / 19px) as the sign-off. Gap 7–8px — deliberately tight, so it reads as one object.

**Misuse:** don't recolour the structure. Don't centre the courses. Don't add a beacon flame, a crosshair, an eye, or a shield. Don't outline it. Don't set the wordmark as two words.

### Palette — the Glass system

The visual story is **a calm morning ground with one lit thing on it.** A muted cool-grey scene, teal-green frosted sheets floating over it, and a highlighter that only ever means signal.

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Ink** | `#142522` | Cool near-black. All headings, key numbers, the dark CTA slab. |
| **Highlighter** | `#eafd35` | **THE accent.** Yellow-green, like a text highlighter — not yellow, not lime. Fills, primary CTAs, the lit course. |
| Highlighter soft | `#e2f463` | Strokes, chart lines, curve. |
| **Deep teal** | `#1e6f5c` | Accent *text* on light ground — the highlighter is unreadable as text. Links, the "tower" in the wordmark. |
| Muted | `#404d4a` | Body copy. |
| Faint | `#5b6a66` | Labels, captions, metadata. |
| On-dark | `#eef6ee` / `#9fb0a8` | Type on the ink slab. |
| Mint | `#3f8f68` | Verified, calm, healthy. |
| Ember | `#c25446` | Threat up, something moved against you. |
| Amber | `#a3701f` | Medium confidence. |
| Sky | `#4f80a3` | Emerging / neutral information. |

**Scene** (the backdrop everything floats on) — neutral cool grey, never converging with the sheets:

```css
radial-gradient(1100px 640px at 80% -14%, #b3bcba 0%, transparent 58%),
radial-gradient(800px 560px at 0% 100%,  #969fa0 0%, transparent 55%),
linear-gradient(160deg, #a7aeae 0%, #9da6a5 52%, #a4acaa 100%)
```

**Glass sheet** — teal-green, translucent, blurred, with a white hairline and a deep soft shadow. Full recipe in `DESIGN-GLASS.md`.

**The two-tone law:** scene is *grey*, glass is *teal-green*. The moment they converge into one sage wash, the illusion dies and it becomes a flat pastel dashboard. This is the single most common way to break the system.

**Accent discipline:** the highlighter appears roughly **once per viewport**. If you can see two highlighter elements at once, one of them is wrong. This is Law 2 made visual.

### Typography

Plus Jakarta Sans throughout. Editorial scale — large, tight headings; generous body.

- Display / H1: 800 weight, `-0.035em` tracking, 1.08 line-height
- Section H2: 800, `-0.03em`
- Body: 400–500, 1.55–1.6 line-height, 15–16.5px
- Labels / eyebrows: 700–800, uppercase, `+0.06em` tracking, 10.5–12px
- Data and timestamps: mono, used sparingly, for anything that is *evidence*

Big type on small screens is not a bug — the app was rebuilt at phone scale in August 2026 specifically because it had been designed at desktop and never brought down.

### Motion

Restrained and purposeful. Nothing bounces.

- Transitions 120–140ms, ease.
- Hover lifts 1px. That's it.
- The one indulgence: the **fog-of-war light sweep** on the homepage. It earns it because it *is* the argument — most of your market is dark, and the light is what reveals it.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` everywhere.

## 8. Applications

- **Product UI** — Glass system, calm, one accent per viewport.
- **Marketing site** — same palette, more contrast allowed, hero screenshots always real product on the demo workspace (never mocked, never a competitor's data).
- **Screenshots** — captured from the live demo workspace at 1440×900 @2x via `scripts/screenshot.mjs`. The demo banner is hidden in captures; the workspace chip stays, because it's honest.
- **Social / OG** — ink ground, highlighter mark, one line of type. Never a stock photo of a person at a laptop.
- **Blog covers** — generated SVG, keyed to the post, in-palette. No stock photography, ever.

## 9. The one-paragraph brand summary

*Watchtower is the watch you don't have to stand. It reads every public trace your competitors leave — pricing, ads, hiring, hostnames, press, reviews — and returns one briefing each morning that says what changed, why it matters, and what to do. Every fact is cited, every gap is disclosed, and the beacon is only ever lit for something real. Scouts gather. The Tower sees.*
