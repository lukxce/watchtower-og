# Watchtower — brand story

## The story

A watchtower exists for one reason: someone stands the watch so everyone else
can sleep. It doesn't shout. It doesn't light the beacon for a fox on the road.
It watches everything, understands what it sees, and lights the fire only when
something real is moving — and when that fire is lit, you trust it, because
the tower has never lied to you.

That's the product. Watchtower stands the watch over your competitors — their
pricing, their launches, their hiring, their ads — reads what it sees, and
brings you conclusions in the morning: what they did, why it matters, what to
do about it. Never a raw pile of alerts. Never a false fire.

Three brand laws that fall out of the story:

1. **The tower reads, you decide.** Everything shown to the customer is a
   conclusion in plain language ("Grin looks to be building something new"),
   never an artifact of how we found it ("Subdomain observed: …"). Detection
   plumbing — hostnames, sitemaps, cert logs — is our business, not theirs.
2. **The beacon is earned.** Loud treatment (color, size, prominence) is
   reserved for things that actually clear an evidence bar. Most of the UI
   stays calm paper; the beacon color means "look here, this is real."
3. **No false fires.** Every conclusion carries its source. Anything
   unverifiable is skipped and logged, and the product says so.

## Voice

Operator to operator. Plain words, short sentences, zero surveillance-thriller
drama and zero data-pipeline jargon. Say "they opened nine technical roles this
month," not "9 signals ingested across the jobs channel."

## Palette — "first light"

The visual story is the watch itself: the deep indigo of the night watch, the
warm bone paper of the morning report, and one amber beacon that only means
signal. Soft cards and rounded shapes stay (the report should feel calm), but
the hues carry the story instead of a generic pastel wash.

| Token | Hex | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Night (ink, dark modules) | `#232038` | the watch itself — headers, text, dark tiles |
| Bone (ground) | `#faf7f2` | the morning report you actually read |
| Beacon (accent) | `#cf7a1f` / soft `#e8a34d` | lit only for real signal — CTAs, live markers, the one loud thing |
| Slate-violet (secondary) | `#6d64b8` | quiet structure — links, secondary chips, map dots |
| Ember (threat up) | `#c25548` | something moved against you |
| Moss (calm / ok) | `#4f9d6b` | verified, healthy, quiet |
| Sky (info) | `#5b87c9` | neutral information |

Dawn gradient hints (amber → lavender) may tint the ground and one featured
tile; saturation stays low everywhere except the beacon.

## What this replaces

The lavender/pink pastel scheme read as a generic wellness dashboard — pleasant
but storyless. Layout, card language, type (Plus Jakarta Sans), pill nav, and
the icon rail all stay; only the hue story changes.
